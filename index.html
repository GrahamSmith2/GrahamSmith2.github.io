Research Project

Skyweaver — Tethered Twin-Aircraft Heavy Lift

MATLAB · 6-DOF Simulation · Guidance & Control

Skyweaver is a heavy-lift concept in which two tethered fixed-wing aircraft orbit a shared payload, acting as one large, lightly-loaded rotor — targeting the 4:1 payload-to-weight goal from DARPA's 2026 Lift Challenge that no entrant met. I built a nonlinear 6-DOF MATLAB simulation of the full system, using NASA FASER's real wind-tunnel-measured airframe data, then designed and compared a cascaded PID autopilot against a rotating-frame LQR. Result: a 25–30 kg payload ceiling set by rudder authority rather than lift or power, with the LQR extending that ceiling by up to 19%.

Animations show the aerodynamic design point; the envelope adds real control-surface limits, which is what sets the payload ceiling.

The coordinated-tether concept itself isn't new — it appears in a 1931 patent (Beauford, US 1,829,474) and was flight-demonstrated in 2023 (Sherbrooke/Georgia Tech, 20 kg payload on two 3.2 kg UAVs). The contribution here is the dynamics simulation and control system built around it, not the concept.

Electric Displacement Catamaran

PEP Competition · Princeton Electric Speedboating

Led the conversion of an existing racing sailing catamaran to electric propulsion: hull selection, decking and flooring, steering system, and full mechanical integration of the 48V powertrain, in-house outboard, 1000A custom battery pack, and electrical system.

Mounted the outboard through the catamaran's existing crossbeam, transferring thrust and torque loads at the system level via a fiberglass-reinforced plywood mount and a CNC-machined metal bracket welded to the crossbeam. Propeller optimization and a small added planing surface took top speed from 12 to 17 mph.
